1.4.2 Module2: Multi-AgentMathematicalReasoning&StepwiseMarking
Module 02 receives the structured steps from Module 01 together with the marking
scheme. Three parallel worker agents validate step correctness, detect the solution
method, and compare the work against the marking scheme respectively. A supervi-
sor agent reconciles their outputs and produces a final structured analysis containing
step_validity,detected_method, assigned_marks, and total_score. The design supports
partialmarking,alternativevalidmethods,andmoretransparentmarkallocationthan
final-answer-onlysystems.

labels,assignedmarks,andmarking-schemecontextintoclearstudent-facingexplana-
tions. Theoutputidentifieswhichstepsarecorrect,explainswheremarkswerelost,and
suggestsspecificimprovements.

2.1 Introduction
As established in Chapter 1, the manual evaluation of handwritten algebra answers
is a time-consuming, inconsistent, and difficult-to-scale process. Existing automated
assessmenttoolspredominantlyfocusonfinal-answerverificationandthereforefailto
capture the student’sstep-by-step reasoningprocess, alternativevalid solutionmethods,
partialmarkingrequirements,andtheneedforcleareducationalfeedback. Thischapter
reviews themost relevantexisting researchand commercialsystems relatedto handwrit-

wherecurrentworkaddressespartsoftheproblem,wherecriticalgapsremain,andhow
thethree-modulesystemproposedbythisprojectaimstofillthosegaps.
2.2 ExistingResearchesandExistingSolutions
Existing work in this domain can be broadly grouped into three thematic categories:
OCR-oriented handwritingand mathematical expression recognition, automated grading
andassessmentsystems,andeducationalfeedbackgeneration. Whileeachcategoryhas
mademeaningfulprogressindividually,noneprovidesacompleteend-to-endpipeline
forhandwrittenstepwisealgebraevaluationwithexplainablefeedback.
Intheareaofhandwrittenmathematicalexpressionrecognition(HMER),Wang,Zhang,
and Jin [3] proposed an attention guidance mechanism that directs the model’s focus
toward structurally critical regions of handwritten expressions, achieving notable im-
provements in recognition accuracy on standard benchmarks. Building on sequential
modellingapproaches,NguyenandVu[8]developedanofflineHMERsystembasedon
a square loss sequence-to-sequence network, demonstrating competitive performance
onisolatedhandwrittenmathematicalexpressiondatasets. Whilebothstudiesrepresent
meaningful advances in symbol-level and expression-level recognition, they operate
exclusivelyonwell-segmented,isolatedmathematicalexpressionsanddonotaccount
forthemixed-contentstructuretypicalofreal examinationanswer sheets. Sreeetal. [2]
applied convolutional and recurrent neural network architectures to general handwriting
recognition, confirming the utility of deep learning for character-level transcription,
but their system is not tailored to algebraic symbols, multi-region exam layouts, or
